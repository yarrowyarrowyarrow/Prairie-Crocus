# V3.64 · Leaf colours, daisy heads and dense spikes (F268)

**The owner:** "Sure do 1-3 next", the first three of the steps
[V3.62](V3.62-the-models-read-again.md) ranked after [V3.63](V3.63-shrubs-you-can-tell-apart.md):

1. **Leaf colour for the rest** (the commonest fault, 88 plants): every native without a
   `leaf_color` is drawn the one green of its plant type.
2. **Daisy heads too small to read** (`flower_size` 49, `flower_count` 14): the asters, fleabanes,
   hawkweed and sunflowers draw their heads at life size and nearly face-up, so a 2 cm head is a
   dot or a sliver from a yard away.
3. **Dense spikes drawn as loose strings** (`flower_arch` 36): goldenrods, milk-vetches,
   hedysarums, licorice, prairie clover, bergamot, owl clover, giant hyssop.

## 1. Leaf colour

169 of the 250 natives had none (the 47 graminoids were read in V3.51, the 34 shrubs in V3.63).
150 of them are plants whose leaves were read; 19 are not (the water plants, the horsetails,
creeping juniper, prickly-pear, ostrich fern).

**How it was read.** A candidate first, measured: the median colour of the foliage pixels (hue
between yellow-green and blue-green, some saturation, neither black nor blown out) in each plant's
picked leaf and whole-plant photographs, mapped through a linear calibration fitted to the 80
colours already read by eye (V3.51, V3.63), which lands within 11 to 16 levels a channel of them.
Then every candidate was looked at beside the photographs and the model and kept or corrected by
eye: the measurement is blind to grey and silver leaves (its saturation floor drops them) and to
a photograph's light. The record is `photo`, as the others are.
