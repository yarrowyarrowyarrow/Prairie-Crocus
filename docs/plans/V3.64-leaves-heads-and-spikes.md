# V3.64 · Leaf colours, daisy heads and dense spikes (F268)

**The owner:** "Sure do 1-3 next", the first three of the steps
[V3.62](V3.62-the-models-read-again.md) ranked after [V3.63](V3.63-shrubs-you-can-tell-apart.md):

1. **Leaf colour for the rest** (the commonest fault, 88 plants): every native without a
   `leaf_color` is drawn the one green of its plant type.
2. **Daisy heads too small to read** (`flower_size` 49, `flower_count` 14): the asters, fleabanes,
   hawkweed and sunflowers draw their heads at life size and nearly face-up, so a 2 cm head is a
   dot or a sliver from a yard away.
3. **Dense spikes drawn as loose strings** (`flower_arch` 36): goldenrods, milk-vetches,
   hedysarums, licorice, prairie clover, bergamot, owl clover, giant hyssop.

## 1. Leaf colour

169 of the 250 natives had none (the 47 graminoids were read in V3.51, the 34 shrubs in V3.63).
150 of them are plants whose leaves were read; 19 are not (the water plants, the horsetails,
creeping juniper, prickly-pear, ostrich fern).

**How it was read.** A candidate first, measured: the median colour of the foliage pixels (hue
between yellow-green and blue-green, some saturation, neither black nor blown out) in each plant's
picked leaf and whole-plant photographs, mapped through a linear calibration fitted to the 80
colours already read by eye (V3.51, V3.63), which lands within 11 to 16 levels a channel of them.
Then every candidate was looked at beside the photographs and the model and kept or corrected by
eye: the measurement is blind to grey and silver leaves (its saturation floor drops them) and to
a photograph's light. The record is `photo`, as the others are.

**What came of it.** All 169 read. **80 measurements kept, 89 corrected by eye**, and the
corrections fell where expected:

- **grey and silver leaves**, which the measurement cannot see: the sageworts (prairie sagewort
  `#b0b8a8`, prairie sage, plains wormwood), the five pussytoes, pearly everlasting, silverweed,
  scarlet butterfly plant, golden bean, hairy golden aster, pale agoseris, prairie crocus, the
  locoweed, the hedysarums, prairie thistle, creeping juniper;
- **blue-green leaves**: smooth aster, dogbane, the meadow-rues, wild chives, nodding onion,
  white spruce and black spruce (measured far too pale), Bebb's willow;
- **dark glossy leaves** the light washed out: bearberry, pink pyrola, balsam poplar, marsh
  marigold, water arum, red baneberry;
- **round-leaved sundew is red** (`#b04a3a`), which is what it is: the measurement found only
  the few green pixels.

**A colour alone is its own reading.** `scripts/leaf_readings.py` refused a row unless the leaves
and habit were read too, which 19 of these (the water plants, the horsetails, creeping juniper,
prickly-pear, ostrich fern) are not. A batch of colours only is now accepted, and it **leaves the
leaf provenance pair as it was**: an unread plant keeps its shared body (P9), and a read one keeps
the citation of the sitting that read its leaves. The colour reaches the scene through
`scene_contract._foliage_color`, which has read `leaf_color` first since V3.51. Schema v105, no
DDL.

## 2. Daisy heads

A new chunk, `html/scene3d/15e-heads.js`, with three stated estimates:

- **a head is drawn at least 3 cm across** (`HEAD_MIN_M`), never more than 1.8 times its width
  (`HEAD_GAIN_MAX`): a 2.5 cm aster at 3 cm, a 1 cm many-flowered aster at 1.8 cm, a 5 cm bergamot
  as it is;
- **a head faces out from the plant and up**, 0.5 to 0.95 radians from face-up (`HEAD_TILT`),
  where it had nodded 0.18 to 0.36 radians in a random direction and was seen edge on;
- **a counted flower cluster of a branched aster is a small spray of heads**
  (`HEADS_PER_CLUSTER`: 3 on stems branched above, 4 throughout, 1 unbranched). The catalogue
  counts an aster's clusters (12 for Lindley's), and a cluster of an aster is several heads.

`_inflorescence` moved into the chunk with them: `15-florets.js` was at 498 of its 500 lines.

## 3. Dense spikes

A wildflower's spike, raceme or panicle of 15 florets or more (`DENSE_FLORETS`) is carried as
**one cut-out card on its stem tip**, the card a shrub's flower clusters have used since V3.49
(`15c-woodybloom.js`): a column of flowers for a spike or raceme, a cone for a goldenrod's
panicle, ending at the tip and never taller than the plant's own share for its flowers. As many
as the catalogue counts clusters. A loose raceme of a few big flowers (meadow blazingstar's eight)
keeps its florets, and so does every head.

Two render tests asked Canada goldenrod for florets (`test_accuracy_render`): they read its card
now, and still hold it under the plant's height.

## What the pictures show

Before (V3.63) and after, through the real viewer, side by side in their flowering months:

- **asters in September**: before, six plants of stems with nothing on them visible from a yard
  away; after, lilac and white heads on Lindley's, smooth, willow and many-flowered asters and
  the hawkweed. Still small at that distance: an aster in a bed is a haze of colour, not daisies.
- **spikes in August**: prairie goldenrod is a golden plume (before, yellow specks round bare
  stems); Canada milk vetch and wild licorice carry cream spikes; purple prairie clover and giant
  hyssop purple ones. The goldenrod reads perhaps too solid; the milk-vetch spikes too blocky.
- **leaf colour close up**: prairie sage and silverweed are now silver; the asters' grey-green.
