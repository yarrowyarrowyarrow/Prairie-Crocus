# V3.49 · Shrubs drawn from their own stems and leaves (F268, third sitting)

**The owner:** "Continue with the 3d model work for the edmonton species."

V3.48 read all 250 native models against photographs and ranked what to fix. Its
second sitting took the first and third items on that list (leaf sizes and
outlines; the wrong kind of body) for the 135 wildflowers, herbs and
groundcovers. The second item was **shrubs as many slender stems, leafy
throughout**, and that is this sitting.

## Where the shrubs stood

The 34 native shrubs scored 3.2 of 10 in the review, none at 7. Their faults,
counted off `data/fetched/model_review.json`:

| fault | shrubs of 34 | what the review saw |
|---|---|---|
| stems | 26 | "trunks as thick as an arm", "a fan of thick square sticks" |
| fruit | 22 | berries missing or hanging in the air |
| leaf size | 19 | "big plates in tiers", "like a cabbage" |
| sparse | 17 | "a grey skeleton with slivers: it reads as a dead bush" |
| no flower | 15 | no flower shape recorded, or single pixels |
| leaf shape | 13 | no maple-like leaf; rose leaflets as blades |

One cause sits under the first, third, fourth and sixth: a shrub was one of
eight baked figures (vase, spreading, mound, thicket, irregular, arching,
prostrate, upright) in a few leaf classes, five to eleven stems with leaf cards
at their ends, stretched to the plant's height and width. A figure eight
species share cannot be a saskatoon and a dogwood both.

## What was run

- The reference photographs are not kept in the repository, so they were
  fetched again: 3,290 photographs for the 250 natives, six at a time, in under
  two minutes (`scripts/model_references.py fetch` then only writes its
  manifest).
- The 34 shrubs and 10 trees were rendered as they stood, and those pictures
  kept as the "before" every later render was laid beside.
- `scripts/leaf_readings.py strips --group shrubs`: the review's picked
  photographs for each shrub in one row, with a **winter** photograph, which is
  the one the stems can be counted on.

## What was read

All 34, one at a time, and recorded with `leaf_readings.py add` (each row
`leaf_data_source = "photo"`, citing the photographs by iNaturalist id):

- **`woody_stems`**, new (schema v99): the stems a grown shrub sends up from
  the ground. 25 for red-osier dogwood, 30 for the sandbar and narrow-leaf
  willows, 18 for saskatoon, 4 for the hawthorn, 5 for silver buffaloberry.
- **Leaf length** changed on 19 and **outline** on 13: eight currants and
  viburnums from `lobed` (an oak's leaf) to `palmately_lobed` (a maple's, the
  word V3.48 added), Labrador tea and silver buffaloberry to `lanceolate` for
  their narrow oblong leaves, raspberry to `trifoliate`.
- **Woody habit** (`branching`) changed on 8: the two wild roses and
  meadowsweet to `suckering` (separate stems over the ground they cover),
  bristly black currant to `arching`, and four low bushes recorded `decurrent`
  (a round-crowned tree's word) to `multi_stem`. The 34 are now 20 clumps, 8
  thickets, 5 arching and one mat (common juniper).
- **Leaf surface** on 4 (Labrador tea's upper side is dark green, its wool is
  underneath; dogwood is matte).
- **Flowers**, only where a photograph contradicted a number: meadowsweet's
  cluster is a steeple (`panicle`), not a flat top; wild gooseberry flowers
  singly or in twos; low-bush cranberry's cluster holds about 20, not 120;
  a raspberry flower is 1.2 cm, not 2.5.

No height, spacing or colour was changed.

## What was decided

**A read shrub gets a body of its own, decided once in Python**
(`src/shrub_habit.py`, a `drawn` block with body `woody`, as the vines, the
pond, the succulents and the wildflowers have). Four forms from the recorded
habit: `clump` (`multi_stem`), `thicket` (`suckering`), `arching`, `mat`
(`prostrate`). A shrub still on the seeder's estimate keeps the baked figure:
drawing a genus guess in a species' own body would state it as a reading (P9).
The 20 non-native shrubs in the catalogue are unread and unchanged.

**The stems.** As many as were counted, from one base or spread over the
footprint as the form says. Their thickness is not recorded and is derived:
`0.019 x sqrt(crown radius x height / stems)` metres at the ground, the same
crown shared between more stems being thinner stems. That gives a dogwood's 25
wands 1.6 cm, a saskatoon's 18 stems 2.5 cm and a hawthorn's 4 trunks 6 cm,
which is what the winter photographs show. Each stem carries children, and
each child its own, until the shrub ends in as many leafy shoots as its leaves
need: the depth of branching follows from the numbers, so four trunks branch
three times and thirty wands once.

**The leaves: a shoot at a time, cut to its outline.** The first build drew
one triangle a leaf. Measured on it: a 4 m saskatoon with 4 cm leaves needs
about 70,000 leaves to half cover its crown; 6,000 triangles buy 4,500; so each
leaf was drawn three times its size and the crown was still a third covered.
Every shrub came out a spray of big triangles. So a leafy shoot is one flat
card the size of the shoot, cut by a mask painted in the viewer to the outline
of the twig and its leaves: the species' leaf outline, its arrangement (pairs
for an opposite-leaved shrub), six to twenty-eight leaves by how narrow the
leaf is, needles as a bottlebrush. The same triangles then carry four to
fourteen times the leaves. What that bought, at the Balanced detail level:

| shrub | height | leaf | triangles | drawn at |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Red Osier Dogwood | 3 m | 8 cm | 4,300 | life size |
| Highbush Cranberry | 3.5 m | 9 cm | 5,500 | 1.07 x |
| Prickly Wild Rose | 1.5 m | 9 cm | 1,650 | life size |
| Sandbar Willow | 3 m | 9 cm | 4,900 | 1.25 x |
| Chokecherry | 5 m | 8 cm | 6,000 | 1.6 x |
| Saskatoon Berry | 4 m | 4 cm | 6,000 | 2.3 x |
| Shrubby Cinquefoil | 1 m | 2 cm | 2,800 | 2.8 x |
| Common Juniper | 1.5 m | 1.2 cm | 3,300 | 3 x (the cap) |

Twenty of the 34 are within a quarter of life size; the small-leaved ones are
enlarged by a factor the build records (`userData.woody.plan.gain`), never more
than three.

Three things about the mask were found by looking, not planned:

- **Nine leaves in two even rows read as one feather-shaped leaf**, and a
  saskatoon looked like a locust. Crowded round a point instead, they ran
  together into one lobed leaf and it looked like a maple. A broad leaf borne
  singly is now a short shoot of six, each on its stalk, no two the same size
  or angle. Narrow leaves and pairs stay along a length of shoot, which is how
  a willow wand and a dogwood twig carry them.
- **The mask keeps its cover as it shrinks.** With the machine's own smaller
  copies a leaf's pixels average below the cut and a shrub goes bare with
  distance, worst on the willows (a tenth of a card is leaf). Each smaller copy
  is rescaled so the same share of its pixels passes.
- **The shadow is cut by the same mask**, or every card shades the ones behind
  it as a whole rectangle.

**Budget by height.** 2,800 triangles for a shrub a metre tall or less, rising
to 6,000 at 4 m (Balanced); 5,000 to 10,000 at Lifelike. The baked figure was
held to 3,600 whatever the shrub, and a tree's largest tier is 6,000.

**A young shrub is a smaller shrub.** Built at 40%, 70% or all of its grown
size, whichever first holds it this year, with fewer stems, so its leaves stay
their own size. Scaled down from the grown body, a second-summer saskatoon had
leaves a centimetre long.

**Flowers and fruit go where the shoots end.** The body names the ends of its
outermost third of shoots (leafy ends in leaf, bare twig ends out of leaf) and
`15b-anchors.js` hands those to the flower and fruit layers.

**Stylised draws none of it**, as it draws none of the wildflowers' bodies.

**The shrub layer moved.** `04-quality.js` was at 899 of 900 lines.
`buildShrubLayer`, `getShrubArch` and their cache went to the new chunk
(`03d-woody.js`) unchanged but for the three lines that ask for a shrub's own
body first; `04-quality.js` is at 815.

## What the pictures show

Every shrub was rendered before and after through the real viewer, in leaf, in
flower and in fruit, and a few in January, in September, at Lifelike and at
Stylised. Saskatoon is a thicket of slender grey stems with small oval leaves
to the ground. Red-osier dogwood is two dozen thin red wands under opposite
leaves at their size, and in January a clump of red wands in the snow, which
is the photograph. The willows are feathery pale masses of wands. The roses
are thickets with leaves of leaflets and their flowers on them. Cinquefoil is
a dense small-leaved dome. The currants are low leafy mounds with lobed
leaves. Common juniper, which was bare brown spokes, is a green mound of
needle sprays. In the whole-yard sheet (`scripts/render_yard.py`) the shrubs
hold their leaves at street distance.

## Found on the way

- A body the plant list does not name is filed under the pond layer. The first
  render drew nothing for that reason; `woody` is now named beside `leafy`.
- Two guards asked every catalogue shrub for a baked variant (as they had
  asked every wildflower until V3.48). A read shrub never asks for one; they
  skip it and count it.
- `VertexColourFlagTest` counted three arguments to `surfaceMaterial`. A
  fourth, the cut-out, is now allowed; the third is still required.

## Left alone, and why

- **Leaf colour by species.** Still one green a genus. Canada buffaloberry is
  drawn silver because silver buffaloberry is; its leaves are dark green
  above. The photographs can supply it; it needs a column and a decision on who
  reads colour (the owner read the flowers'), so it stays its own increment.
- **Catkins.** The willows, alders and the hazel still have none in April.
- **Width.** A shrub is as wide as 1.5 times its planting spacing, so a
  raspberry is 0.9 m across at 1.5 m tall, where the photographs show a thicket
  as wide as tall. Spacing is the planner's number and was not touched.
- **Trees, grasses, sedges, vines.** The same technique would serve the tree
  crowns (the review's sixth item) and they now look crude beside the shrubs.
- **The baked shrub models** are unchanged and still serve the 20 unread
  shrubs. Rebuilt, they would bake variants only read shrubs name.
- **The scores** in the review file were not re-read, as in the second sitting.

## Not verified

- **On a real GPU.** Everything was drawn by Chromium's software renderer. The
  cut-out is an alpha test, which every graphics card supports, but its edges
  and the shimmer of far leaves under motion were not seen on one.
- **Frame rate.** A big shrub is up to 6,000 triangles where the baked figure
  was up to 3,600, and its leaves now discard pixels. Not measured on the
  owner's laptop (Ctrl+Shift+F in the 3D window shows frames a second).
- **In the app's window.** Checked through the probe pages the render gates
  use, not the Qt window.
- **My readings.** One reader, 250 px photographs. A stem count off a winter
  photograph is a count of what shows, on a plant whose age nobody knows.
