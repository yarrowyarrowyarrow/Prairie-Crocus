# V3.50 · Trees and vines drawn from their photographs (F268, fourth sitting)

**The owner:** "Go ahead with the trees and vines in this next increment. Show
me what you are capable of."

V3.48 read all 250 Edmonton natives against photographs, V3.48's second
sitting rebuilt the 135 wildflowers, and V3.49 the 34 shrubs. The trees were
the next item on the ranked list, and the vines the plants the review scored
lowest (2 of 10, every one).

## Where they stood

From `data/fetched/model_review.json`:

| | review | what it said |
|---|---|---|
| 10 trees | mean 4.0, none at 7 | every one `crown`: "a very long pole with a dozen leaf clumps near the top" (aspen), "a white pole with four upright leaders" (paper birch), "solid flat plates with the spruce's pencil tip" (tamarack), "a bottle brush" (jack pine), "a narrow tuft on a square-sectioned post" (balsam poplar), "a fan of straight rods under a flat cap, like a parasol" (water birch) |
| 5 vines | 2 of 10, all five | "a wisp at the host's foot", "a few threads of tiny ladder leaves", flowers "specks in the air" |

## What was read

The review's picked photographs of each, with a winter one, in strips
(`scripts/leaf_readings.py strips --group trees`), all 15 read and recorded
with `leaf_readings.py add` (each row `leaf_data_source = "photo"`, citing the
photographs).

**The trees needed three things nothing recorded**, so schema v100 adds them:

| column | what it says | the ten |
|---|---|---|
| `crown_shape` | the outline of a tree grown in the open | conical (white spruce, tamarack), spire (black spruce), oval (aspen, balsam poplar, paper birch, pin cherry), rounded (Bebb's willow, water birch), irregular (jack pine) |
| `crown_clear` | the share of the height that is bare trunk below the lowest live limb | 0.02 white spruce, 0.05 black spruce, 0.1 the willow and water birch, 0.15 tamarack, 0.3 paper birch, 0.35 pin cherry, 0.4 jack pine, 0.45 balsam poplar, 0.55 aspen |
| `limb_habit` | which way the main limbs go | ascending (the poplars, aspen, birches, cherry, willow), spreading (white spruce, tamarack, jack pine), drooping (black spruce) |

The trunk count goes in `woody_stems` (V3.49's column): one for most, two for
the paper birch, five for Bebb's willow, six for the water birch.

Leaves: length on 8 of the 15, outline on 2 (pin cherry stays lanceolate; the
clematis's is `trifoliate`, three heart-based leaflets, not the pinnate leaf it
was recorded with), surface on 2 (the peavine and the honeysuckle are
glaucous). Flowers, only where a photograph contradicted the number or there
was none: catkins (`flower_arch` spike, no petals) on the balsam poplar, aspen
and water birch; the pin cherry's cluster is an umbel of about six, not a
raceme of thirty; the clematis's lantern is 5 cm of four sepals; the vines'
cluster counts. **`flower_form` was not touched**: a poplar's is `none`, which
the pollinator logic reads as wind-pollinated, and it stays so. No colour was
changed; see the end for the ones the photographs disagree with.

## What was decided

**A read tree gets a body of its own, decided once in Python**
(`src/tree_habit.py`, a `drawn` block with body `crown`), drawn by a new
viewer chunk (`html/scene3d/03e-crowns.js`). It is V3.49's shrub body with a
trunk and limbs under it, so the forking, the leafy shoots as cards cut to
their leaves' outline, the budget and the flower ends are that chunk's,
given this one's outline:

- **Trunks**: round (eight faces, where a shrub's are three), tapering, a
  little crooked on the irregular pine; a rounded crown forks into three
  leaders above its bole. Thickness is the one stated estimate
  `TRUNK_PIPE x height` shared between trunks: a 20 m aspen 38 cm across at
  the base, an 8 m pin cherry 15 cm.
- **Limbs** from the top of the bare bole up each trunk, leaving it at the
  habit's angle (ascending 46°, spreading 7°, drooping 16° below level with
  the tip turned up), the upper ones rising more; a conifer's in whorls of
  four every 40 cm (or a fortieth of the tree), which is what turned the
  first build's pagoda tiers into a spruce.
- **The crown's outline** by shape (`crownEnvelope`), nothing below the bole,
  and every branch kept inside it.
- **Needles as the species carries them** (decided in Python as `spray`): a
  spruce's flat fronds of twiglets needled all round (`frond`), a pine's long
  needles in tufts at the shoot ends (`tuft`), a tamarack's soft rosettes on
  spur shoots (`rosette`). Each is a painted mask a branchlet long.
- **A broadleaf tree's spray is a branchlet**: three of the species' leafy
  shoots on a forked twig, one card. A shrub's single shoot could not cover a
  20 m crown inside the budget.
- **Budget by height**: 6,000 triangles at 5 m to 14,000 at 20 m (Balanced),
  10,000 to 24,000 at Lifelike. The baked crowns' largest was 6,000.

**A read vine keeps the body `vine_habit` gives it** (climbing what it
touches) and gets its own leafy shoots along its stems
(`html/scene3d/22b-vineleaves.js`): the species' leaf, in its arrangement, at
its size, cut to its outline, facing out from the crown it climbs, and lying
flat where it runs along the ground (the accuracy gate caught the first build
standing 50 cm tall in the open, past the 30 cm a vine with nothing to climb
may reach).

**Flowers.** The flower cards V3.49 gave the shrubs (`15c-woodybloom.js`) now
serve the trees and the vines too: catkins on the poplar, aspen, water birch
and Bebb's willow in April, **hanging** from the twigs on the poplars and
birches and standing on the willow (`catkins_hang`, a family character
decided in Python: Salicaceae's poplars and Betulaceae's catkins hang, a
willow's stand); the cherry's umbels; the vines' racemes and lanterns. A
card body passes the flower layer on its colour alone, so a catkin recorded
`flower_form: none` is drawn without the word the pollinator logic reads
being changed. A tree's clusters take the whole of the larger-than-life
allowance tall woody plants already had (`woodyBloomGain`, up to 2.5 at 6 m)
and stand clear of the leaves.

**A narrow leaf borne singly clusters.** The cherry's lance leaves in two
even rows read as a fern. They now take the short-shoot layout the saskatoon
took in V3.49 (`ratio >= 0.15`, where it was 0.3). Grass-like `linear` leaves
(the willows) stay in rows. This also reaches four shrubs with lance leaves
(meadowsweet, basket willow, Labrador tea, silver buffaloberry), and
meadowsweet in particular reads better for it.

## Found on the way

- **The pond layer took the new body again**: `buildPlants` files any body
  it does not name under the pond, and the first render drew nothing. V3.49
  wrote that down; it happened anyway. `crown` is named now, and
  `test_tree_habit` holds the line.
- A variable named `k` in the leaf placer shadowed the loop's `k`: every tree
  threw on its first build. Caught by the probe's error watcher.
- The reference sheets frame a vine's host, so a vine on the new dense
  dogwood is invisible in them. The vines were judged from close renders of
  their own side; the sheet's camera is left as it is.

## Left alone, and why

- **Width.** A tree is 1.5 times its planting spacing wide, which is the
  planner's number: Bebb's willow and the water birch are narrower than their
  photographs. Not touched.
- **Cones** on the spruces, the jack pine and the tamarack, and the clematis's
  feathery seed heads: no fruit shape for either is drawn yet.
- **Leaf colour by species**, still one green a genus.
- **The pin cherry's blossom** is faint: the clusters are there (500 cards in
  May) but small against its crown. A sheet of white needs more than its
  recorded count of umbels; left until the flower counts are read.
- **The jack pine** reads as open and lumpy, which the photographs show, but
  its tufts look more like leaf clumps than needles at a distance.
- **Grasses, sedges, rushes and the water plants** are next on the list (seed
  heads by form).

## Not verified

- **On a real GPU**, as before: software rendering only.
- **Frame rate.** A grown aspen is 14,000 triangles where the baked crown was
  up to 6,000. A yard holds a few trees; not measured on the owner's laptop
  (Ctrl+Shift+F in the 3D window shows frames a second).
- **My readings.** One reader, 250 px photographs; a bare-bole share read off
  a photograph of one tree is an estimate of the species.
