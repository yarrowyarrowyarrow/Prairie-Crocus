# V3.48 · The plant models, read against photographs (F268)

**The owner:** "Run `model_references.py` status, then all, look through every photo per plant,
decide which ones are good references, and start improving the 3D models on the next branch. This
is a deviation from the other 3D work we are doing but I would like the Edmonton plants to be
modelled more accurately."

This is the first time the models have been compared with photographs of each species. Six
earlier audits compared one exemplar per archetype, or compared nothing
([SPRITE_AUDIT.md](../SPRITE_AUDIT.md) says so of its sixth pass). It is a start, as asked: every
sheet is read and recorded, four faults that many plants shared are fixed, and the rest is
ranked for the sessions that follow.

## What was run

- `python scripts/model_references.py status`: 250 plants, all 250 in the candidates file.
- `all`: 3,288 photographs fetched (two more could not be fetched), 558 pictures
  of the models, 250 sheets. The fetch ran at about 7 s a plant, half an hour; fetched six at a
  time it took 90 seconds.
- Every sheet read, one plant at a time, and recorded with `scripts/model_review.py`.

## The sheets were changed before they were read

The first sheets could not be judged from, so `model_references.py` changed first:

| was | now | why |
|---|---|---|
| every plant drawn in July | a wildflower in the middle of its flowering; a tree, shrub or vine in July, with a second picture in flower when July is outside it, and a third in fruit where a fruit colour is recorded | Prairie Crocus flowers in April. In July 91 of the 250 natives are out of flower, and a flower is most of what a wildflower looks like |
| the camera level with half the plant's height | tilted down 20° on anything up to 1.2 m, level from 3 m | level, a knee-high plant shows the undersides of its flowers, which nobody standing in a yard sees |
| scenes read from `html/sprite_gallery_scenes.json` | built from the catalogue as it stands, by the gallery's own builder | a changed row shows on the next render with no other step, and *Crataegus chrysocarpa* (no scene in the file) is drawn like the rest |
| the model in two 300 px tiles above the photographs | the side view four times the area, the photographs round it | the model is what is being judged |
| nothing about the data | the recorded morphology in the header | a fault can then be told apart as the data's or the drawing's |

## What a reading records

`data/fetched/model_review.json`, one entry a plant (committed; the photographs and sheets are
not):

- **refs**: the photographs that are good references, by what each is good for (habit, flower,
  leaf, fruit, winter, bark), kept by iNaturalist photograph id. 2,771 picks of the 3,288. A
  rebuilt sheet frames them.
- **score**, 0 to 10, the sprite audit's question: *would someone who knows this plant recognise
  it, and tell it from its neighbours?* 7 and up means the model does its job.
- **faults**, from one vocabulary of 24 (`model_review.py faults`), so they can be counted.
- **note**: what the plant looks like in the photographs, then what the model gets wrong. Form and
  colour only (P12).

## What was fixed

Four changes, each one cause reaching many plants, each seen before and after on the plants named.

1. **A petal lets light through** (`15-florets.js`, `PETAL_TRANSLUCENCY`). A flower seen from the
   side was half bright faces and half near-black backs: blue flax half navy, marsh marigold half
   brown. Petals take the leaf-light rule of V2.94 at 0.8. Every modelled flower.
2. **Low plants are not baked dark** (`build_all.LOW_AO`). Herbs, grass tufts and groundcover
   kept the bake the tree crowns gave up in V2.94: the gradient fell to 0.55 at the ground and
   rays reached 60% of the plant. A rosette's leaves, a mat and the base of every tussock are low.
   Rebaked with the crowns' rule, a little lighter. The seven herb and three layer models;
   nothing but their shade changed, except as below.
3. **A grass blade lets light through** (`LEAF_TRANSLUCENCY.blade`, 0.5). V2.94 left blades
   opaque on the strength of their lifted normals; the baked blades have none, and half of every
   tuft drew black. `test_leaf_light` asserted they were opaque and now asserts the opposite, with
   the reason.
4. **Flowers and fruit sit on the plant** (new chunk `15b-anchors.js`). A herb's go on its stem
   tips, the highest points of its drawn body, tallest first. A shrub's or tree's go on the
   outside of its crown, all round and from a quarter of its height up; out of leaf, on its twigs.
   Fruit goes where the flowers were. A woody plant's flower count comes from its crown's size
   (it was nine for every shrub, or a forb's recorded 14), up to 90, and its flowers are drawn up
   to 2.5 times life size on a tall shrub, the trade V2.88 made inside a cluster: at life size a
   4 m saskatoon's flowers were single pixels. That factor is an estimate and the chunk says so.

### Found on the way

- **The herb models were stale against the catalogue.** A model's variants are the leaf classes
  the catalogue uses. The catalogue has lost species since they were last built, so the rebuild
  bakes 24 clump, 32 erect and 8 groundcover variants where 26, 34 and 9 were shipped. The
  manifest's entries for the ten rebuilt models are replaced with them.
- **A subset build writes no manifest**, so models rebuilt with `only=` and copied in mismatch the
  shipped manifest (the first attempt failed `test_model_assets` three ways). Build everything to
  a scratch folder and copy the models wanted with their manifest entries.
- **Six other models do not rebuild byte for byte** on `bpy` 4.2.23 (bat, beetle, bird, fly,
  mammal, the snag). They were left as shipped; the manifest's generator line still says 4.2.0.

## Left alone, and why

- **Every body.** No stem, leaf or crown was redrawn. That is the larger share of the faults and
  the next sessions' work, in this order by plants reached:
  1. leaf sizes and outlines from the photographs (112 and 87 plants): data first, then two new
     outlines (a lobed maple-like leaf; a twice-divided leaf) and the three-part and palmate leaf;
  2. shrubs as many slender stems, leafy throughout (34);
  3. leafy-stemmed wildflowers taken off the rosette body, and the meadow-rues and bedstraws
     given leaves on their stems (44);
  4. seed heads by form, at their size, and the four graminoids drawing daisies (43);
  5. dense spikes, plumes and flat-topped heads drawn solid (34);
  6. tree crowns: birch, aspen, tamarack, the pines.
- **Per-species leaf colour.** Nothing records it; the photographs can supply it. A schema change,
  so its own increment.
- **Catkins.** Ten willows, alders and the hazel are bare sticks in April.
- **The species data.** 14 plants carry a number the photographs contradict (`data` in the review).
  Changing seed data needs a schema bump; they wait for the leaf pass.
- **Stylised** is untouched: it draws billboards, not florets.

## Not verified

- **On a real GPU.** Everything was drawn by Chromium's software renderer. Colour and light can
  differ on a graphics card; shapes and positions do not.
- **Frame rate.** A big shrub in flower now draws up to 90 clusters where it drew 14. In May a
  yard of shrubs costs more than it did. Not measured on the owner's laptop (Ctrl+Shift+F in the
  3D window shows it).
- **In the app's window.** Checked through the probe page the render gates use, not the Qt window.
- **My reading of the photographs.** One reader, 300 px tiles, no flora beside them. Where a note
  names a number (a leaf's length, a head's width) it is read off the photograph against a hand or
  a neighbouring plant, not measured.

python scripts/model_references.py all --only "Saskatoon Berry"   # its sheet, rebuilt
