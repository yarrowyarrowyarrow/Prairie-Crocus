# V3.54 · The example is a real lot, planned as the app plans one (F269)

*The owner, 17 November 2026: "There was a example design done by fable that had a house and a
walkway and all that, which I quite liked. Can we find that and replace the example design with
this one." Then, asked where it should sit: "53.4875019,-113.5422427 is good for coordinates of an
example property but the design will need some rework to reflect the reality of this property."*

## Which design

The design they liked is the 3D programme's **review lot**, built by the sessions that did
V3.43 to V3.46 (`scripts/render_yard.py`, `review_lot`): a scanned house and garage, two
neighbours, two trees, a front bed with a walk and a driveway beside it, a back patio, lawn, two
back beds, a fence and a hedge, planned by Plan My Yard front then back. Every 3D sheet since
V3.43 shows it.

It could not ship as it was. **An example with a house opens on its own lot** (V3.38's rule,
`onboarding.example_position`: drawn at someone's pin, its house would sit on their real one), and
the review lot sits on a real residential block in south Edmonton, so every copy of the app would
have drawn an invented house over a stranger's home. The owner gave a lot to use instead, and
asked that the design follow that property.

So **what is kept is the design's kind, not its geometry**: a real city lot with its house,
garage, walk, deck, trees and fences, beds front and back, planted by Plan My Yard front then back,
exactly as the review lot was made.

## Reading the property

No session can see a person's yard, but this one can read the same sources the app does. Six hosts
were added to the environment's allowlist (approved by the owner on the card): the three Overpass
servers and OpenFreeMap's tiles (the app's *Scan this area*), the City of Edmonton's open data, and
Esri's imagery (the app's satellite layer).

- **The pin is in the street.** The lot is the one beside it on the west side: the City's property
  points put that house's lot centre 2 m north of the pin, and the lots there are 15.2 m apart.
  East of the pin is a church parking lot. No address is written anywhere in the repo.
- **Buildings** from OpenStreetMap, by its map tiles (no Overpass server answered): the house, the
  garage and the neighbours' houses and garages. Their shapes match the photograph and **sit 2.2 m
  west of it**, a common offset between the two; they were moved onto the photograph, since the
  example opens on it. The tiles carry no height: 5.5 m for a house (bungalows) and 3.5 m for a
  garage, stated estimates.
- **Trees** from the canopy-height map, checked against the photograph. The map reads one crown as
  a cluster of peaks and missed the spruce on the south line out front; the photograph also
  showed that its "front-yard spruce" stands next door. Five trees: the front spruce on the
  south line (8 m, estimated; the map missed it), the neighbours' spruces leaning over the north
  side front and back (9 and 11 m), and two broad trees at the lane (11 and 10 m, measured).
- **The lot**, 15.2 m by about 41.7 m: the fences in the photograph, the back of the sidewalk and
  the lane.
- **What is on it**, from the photograph: the front walk to the door, the deck along the back
  wall, the back lawn south of the garage.

## The design

Drawn on that: a front bed north of the walk and a small bed south of it (clear of the spruce),
the walk, the deck, the back lawn, a sunny bed along the garage's south wall and a bed along the
south fence, and the two side fences. Then planted by Plan My Yard **as the simple app runs it**:
native here and pollinators (`local_native`, `pollinator`), *Fill it now* (70%), the front with
the street to the east, then the back.

The first run used the first-run goals the review lot used, and `make_example.py` refused it:
Little Bluestem and Silvery Lupine are not on the reviewed Edmonton list. That refusal is the
example doing its job; the simple app's own goals were the right ones.

The bed along the south fence was drawn as the "shady bed". The plan put sun plants there and
V3.52's shade step changed nothing in it, which is correct: the neighbour's house is not south of
it, only their yard. It is called the bed by the south fence.

**What the Overview says of it**, as the person opening it will read it:

- Recorded as food for 160 kinds of bee, 127 of butterfly and moth and 2 of bird. Few birds.
- In flower from March to October; all 16 kinds native around Edmonton.
- About three quarters of the beds covered once grown (67 of 80 m²).
- From the street, 4 of 4 signs of care.
- **Plants about $3,600 to $7,300, and 680 to 1,400 hours of work in the first year.**

399 plants of 16 kinds: Wild Strawberry, Fireweed, the two asters, Stiff Goldenrod, Meadow
Blazingstar, Early Blue Violet, Wild Vetch, Wild Raspberry, Oval-leaf Milkweed, Pussy and Basket
Willow, Giant Hyssop, Prairie Crocus, Golden Alexanders and June Grass. V3.52's shade step chose
Stiff Goldenrod where the house shades the ground.

## The example carries lines now

Until now an example could carry areas, buildings, trees and structures, not a fence or a hedge.
`region.EXAMPLE_LINES` (`hedgerow`: style, species, colour, width, spacing, length) with
`points`; `make_example.py` keeps them, `onboarding.example_features` draws them as Draw › Shape
stores one, and a line with fewer than two points is refused. `tests/test_make_example.py` carries
a fence through the round trip.

## The example says which side the street is on

The first render of the example in 3D opened **staring into a neighbour's wall from a metre
away**: the example format carried no street side, so the 3D window assumed the south and stood
its street view between two houses. A person's design keeps the side they told Plan My Yard
(`site_config.street_side`); the example now does too (`street_side`, one of the four compass
sides, kept by `make_example.py` and set by `build_example_project`). With it the window opens on
the sidewalk looking at the front walk, the bed and the house, and the street is drawn
([the example in 3D](../3d/example_v354.jpg)). *From the house* still looks out over pale ground,
and is the weakest of the four views.

## Tests that changed, and why

Five tests read the old example's particular content, and say so now:

- `test_local_flora`: the example's centre was the map's Edmonton centre (downtown); it is the
  owner's lot now, held within 15 km of it.
- `test_one_spacing`: the Wild Bergamot acceptance used the example because it held two; they are
  planted beside its first plant now, so the path is still the example's.
- `test_yard_overview`: the readings above in place of the old example's ("about a third",
  the Cedar Waxwing, no October flower); its hours check passes the example's structures, as the
  item counts them (the old example had none); and the street item's own step is checked with
  every sign of care taken away, since this example has all four.

## Not settled, for the owner

- **The cost and the hours.** 399 plants at *Fill it now* is what Plan My Yard makes for 80 m² of
  beds, and the review lot was the same. As the first thing a person opens it may read as a lot.
  Planned at *Over two or three summers* (40%) it would be fewer plants for less. The owner's
  call; the plan here does what the app does.
- **The hours figure itself** (2 to 3.5 hours a plant in the first year) comes from
  `maintenance_calendar` and looks high for a bed of wildflowers. Not changed here: it is a
  headline number on every design.
- **The building heights and the missed spruce's height** are estimates; the owner knows the
  property.
