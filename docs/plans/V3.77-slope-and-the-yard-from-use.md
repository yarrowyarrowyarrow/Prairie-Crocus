## What each item is in the code

### 1. Slope: the *Slope grid* number, and where the labels land (→ F289)

**The number does something, but almost nothing you can see, and in Edmonton it throws the LiDAR
away.** Site › Slope's *Slope grid* box (5–60 m, default 30 m) is the spacing of the elevation grid
the slope colour ramp is made from. What it touches depends on where the yard is:

- **In Edmonton** (the owner's case) the contour lines come straight from the City's 0.5 m LiDAR
  contours (`terrain.fetch_edmonton_contours`) and **do not use the grid at all**. Only the ramp
  does: the contours' vertices are blended onto the grid (`terrain._grid_from_contours`).
- **Elsewhere** both the contours and the ramp come from the grid, from NRCan's 1–2 m LiDAR where
  it exists and Open-Meteo's 30 m elevation where it does not.

The ramp is a picture **one pixel per grid cell**, stretched over the area by the browser, which
smooths it. Measured with the app's own functions on a yard-sized view (synthetic 0.5 m contours
over a gentle slope with a 1.5 m mound):

| Slope grid | Ramp picture, 160 × 100 m view | Same, 60 × 40 m yard | Steepest slope it reads |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 m | 4 × 3 px | 2 × 2 px | 1.8% |
| **30 m (default)** | **6 × 4 px** | **3 × 2 px** | **1.5%** |
| 20 m | 9 × 6 px | — | 2.7% |
| 10 m | 17 × 11 px | 7 × 5 px | 5.4% |
| 5 m (the box's floor) | 33 × 21 px | 13 × 9 px | 8.4% |

So moving 30 to 25 or 20, which is what the box's 5 m step invites, changes a 6 × 4 blur into a
9 × 6 blur: nobody would see it. And on 0.5 m LiDAR the 30 m default reads the mound's slope at a
fifth of what the 5 m grid reads. The box's tooltip says "Open-Meteo's underlying DEM is 30 m
native", which is not true in Edmonton or wherever NRCan has flown. `terrain.generate_terrain` has
its own better defaults (5 m in Edmonton, 15 m elsewhere), and they never run, because the panel
always sends the box's number.

**The labels.** *Label every 5th contour* is drawn by `drawAutoContours` (`html/map/06-overlays.js`)
and has two faults:

1. **It is not every 5th line.** The rule is "every 5 m of height above the lowest line"
   (`Math.round((elev - minE) * 2) % 10 === 0`), which is every 5th line only at a 1 m interval:

   | Interval | Lines labelled (starting at 668 m) |
   |---|---|
   | 0.5 m (Edmonton's default) | 668, 673, 678: **every 10th** |
   | 1 m | 668, 673, 678, 683: every 5th |
   | 0.25 m | 668, **672.75, 673**: a rounding pair a quarter metre apart |
   | 2 m | 668, 678, 688: every 5th |

   On a yard with under 5 m of relief, which is most yards here, **only the lowest line is
   labelled**, and the lowest line lies at the edge of the area by construction.
2. **Each label sits at the middle vertex of the line's longest piece, as fetched.** The City's
   lines come whole (the open-data query returns every line that crosses the area) or, from the
   offline pack, every line of every tile the area touches (`terrain_store.get_edmonton_contours`).
   Neither is cut to the area. That is why the lines run past the boundary to the edge of the view
   and beyond (the owner is fine with that), and why a label's "middle" is usually somewhere else
   entirely: off the ramp, off the screen.

**Proposed (F289):** labels on every 5th line counted from round heights (at 0.5 m: 670.0, 672.5,
675.0, so the words become true at every interval), each placed where its line is inside the
boundary when there is one, else inside the ramp's area, and repeated along a long line (about one
per 200 px of line on screen), never outside the ramp. **The *Slope grid* box goes** (decision D1):
the grid follows the source, the finest the 10,000-cell limit allows on LiDAR (about 2 m on a
200 m view, never finer than 1 m) and 30 m on Open-Meteo, and the status line says which ("from the
City's 0.5 m LiDAR, sampled every 2 m"). That is the "slight simplification": one fewer control that
did nothing visible, and a ramp that shows the yard. Measure the blending's time at 2 m before
building it; it is a Python loop over every cell.

### 2. Several yard things at once (→ F290)

Today a **placed object stays armed**, so ten bee logs are ten clicks; nothing lays them out. An
**area** (a raised bed) is one outline, rectangle or circle per press of *Draw on the map*. Plants
and communities have *Single*, *Row*, *Grid*, *Circle* and *Fill area* (`placement_controls.PATTERNS`;
Row, Grid and Circle only with Advanced tools on), and the geometry is already general:
`_rowPositions`, `_gridPositions`, `_circlePositions` in `html/map/03-plants.js` take two clicks and
a spacing and return points.

**Proposed (F290):** the same pattern choices on the Yard tab for objects and for areas placed as a
rectangle or a circle. The spacing is the thing's own size plus a gap the person sets (a row of
3 × 1.2 m raised beds with 0.6 m paths between, a fill of 1.2 m bee logs 2 m apart); the map draws
the footprints under the cursor before the second click (as `08-footprint.js` does for plants); the
lot is **one undo step** and one placement group, so it can be selected and moved together. A row
of rectangles turns with its row (each bed's long side along it, or across it: one choice). Fill
area lays objects on the same even fill plants use, at their size plus the gap. Which patterns the
simple app shows is D5.

### 3. A path drawn as a line (→ F291)

A path is an area today: an outline corner by corner, a rectangle or a circle, which is the
"cumbersome" part for a winding path. Lines already exist (a swale is drawn along its middle at a
width), and the keep-out already reads a line as its strip (`yard_kinds.line_strips`).

**Proposed (F291):** *Place it as: Line, along its middle* for a path, with a width (1.0 m to start,
`SHAPE_SIZE_M`'s path width). **Stored as the strip's outline**, an ordinary path area, so the
Overview's planting area, the bare-ground and mulch figures, the 3D surface and the planner all
read it with no change, with the centre line and the width kept on the feature so it can be edited
as a line later. (Storing it as a line kind instead would mean teaching every area reader about
lines, for nothing the person sees.) Joins are mitred and the ends square; a path that crosses
itself is refused, as an outline is (`outline_check`).

### 4. Lawn conversion (→ F292, decision D3)

The owner is mostly right, and the numbers say exactly where. The five *Lawn conversion* kinds:

| Kind | What reads it today |
|---|---|
| Lawn — to convert | Plan My Yard plants it (a bed since V3.28); Site prep counts it as sod to clear; the PDF's conversion schedule counts it as lawn left; the Habitat page's lawn contrast counts its area |
| Restoration — Year 1, Year 3, Established native | Planted like a bed; drawn in 3D as seeded ground, a young meadow, a meadow; counted as "converted" in the schedule and the lawn contrast |
| Existing remnant | Kept out of (wild ground to keep) |

What **nothing** does: a *Year 3* or *Established* area does not tell the timeline or the 3D that
the plants in it are three years old or grown (they still grow from planting day), Plan My Yard
plants an *Established native* area as if it were bare, and no stage changes what is suggested. So
four of the five are labels with a colour. Two meanings are real and worth keeping: **this ground
is lawn now** (it changes the prep: the sod comes off first, P8) and **this is wild ground to keep**.

**Recommended:** retire the group; *Bed* gains "What is there now: lawn / bare ground", which feeds
Site prep and the conversion schedule as *Lawn — to convert* does now; *Wild ground to keep* (the
remnant) moves to *For wildlife*; old designs convert on open (`yard_upgrade`: *Lawn — to convert*
→ a bed on lawn, the three stages → a bed, the remnant → wild ground to keep), never an undo step.
The conversion schedule (F17) and the lawn contrast (F10) keep working from the bed's answer.

### 5. Selecting everything (→ F279, extended)

What the map can select (`_marqueeHitTest`, `selectAll`, `_shownOnMap` in `html/map/01-core.js` and
`04-tools.js`): plants, boundaries, placed objects (and the existing trees and buildings marked by
hand, which are objects), drawn areas (and scanned buildings and tree crowns, which are areas),
measurements, the sun path. **Not**: every line, that is **fences, walls, hedges and swales**. A
line is in no selection path at all: a click does not select it, the selection box and Select All
do not catch it, it cannot be dragged or have its corners moved, and its only action is the
right-click *Remove*. The map's notes (annotations) are not selectable either. F279 (a drag moves
every selected kind) is the section this belongs to; the owner's item adds lines as a kind to
select, move, edit by its corners and delete with the rest, one undo step.

### 6. Buildings in two places, and the shade path (→ F278, extended, decision D4)

There are **four ways to put a building on the map**: *Scan this area* and *Import Building
Outlines* (Site › Features), *Draw building outline* and *Mark building* (Site › Features, Advanced
tools), and Placement › Yard › *Building*. All four make a shade-casting outline the planner keeps
out of; two of them (Mark building and Yard › Building) disagree in form (a box at a size versus an
outline). An **existing tree** can only be scanned or marked on Site › Features (Advanced tools).
Sun & Shade has its own *Existing features ▸* list with *Scan this area* beside it (V3.37). F278,
already open from the V3.69 tester ("the order of the tabs should have existing features before
sun/shade"), is this.

**Recommended (the owner's "all under the Yard tab"):** the Yard list opens with a first group,
**Already here**: *Scan this area* (buildings and trees, as now), *Building*, *Tree*, *Hedge*,
*Fence*, *Wall*, with one sentence at its head: "Shade comes from what is already here: add it
first." Site › Features retires (its advanced imports and their settings move into that group
behind Advanced tools). Sun & Shade's first line says what casts the shade it shows and links to
*Already here* ("Shade from 2 buildings and 5 trees. Add what is missing on the Yard tab"). One
way to put a building down, and the order said where it matters.

### 7. Shade on the map, from what the design adds (→ F293)

The map's shade (`shade.casters_from_project`, drawn as shapes by `shadow_polygons_payload`) and
the 3D (real-time shadows of every drawn thing) disagree:

| Thing | 3D | Map's shade today |
|---|---|---|
| A building, drawn or scanned; an existing tree | casts | casts |
| A hedge, a wall, a board fence | casts | casts (V3.74–V3.75) |
| A tree or shrub in the design, 2 m or more grown | casts, at the year shown | casts, **always fully grown** |
| A shrub under 2 m grown | casts | **never** |
| A snag (3.5 m), a bee hotel (1.6 m), a rain barrel, a compost bin | casts (the models' heights) | **never**: the objects' catalogue (`src/db/structures.py`) gives them no height at all |
| After placing a plant, an object or a line | follows at once | **the shade on screen stays as it was** until *Show shade* is pressed again: only a drawn area, a corner moved and a scan redraw it (`_refresh_shade_if_active`) |

The last row is most likely what the owner saw: plant a tree with the shade showing and nothing
changes on the map.

**Proposed (F293):** the shade on screen follows every edit (plants, objects, lines, moves,
deletes, undo), debounced as `follow_design` does for the 3D window, and drawn off the main thread
as now; every object has one height, read by both the map and the 3D (the model manifest's heights
are the only ones today); woody plants count from 1 m grown, not 2 m. **What the design's own plants
add is a choice** (D6): recommended, one switch on Sun & Shade, *Include what you are planting*, on,
with the plants counted fully grown and the words saying so, because the planner reads only the
shade of what is already there and a person deciding where a shade plant goes wants to see both.
**Two readers move with the caster list and must be measured before and after**: Plan My Yard's
sun/part/shade cells (`llm_design` reads `shade_grid_for_design`) and the timeline's fixed casters
(`static_casters_from_project`, which would then count a snag). `map_events.py` is at 2,050 of its
2,100 lines: the refresh goes in a flow module.

### 8. The fire pit that became a shed (→ F288)

**Not reproduced.** A fire pit placed as the Yard tab places it reaches the 3D as `fire_pit`
(checked through `build_scene`), the model file holds stones, ash and logs, and the real viewer
draws it as a fire pit ([render](V3.77-fire-pit-in-3d.jpg), `scripts/render_structures.py`). The
conversion on open leaves it alone. **The likeliest cause** is the viewer's placeholder: an object
whose model has not loaded is drawn as a box (`buildStructures`, `05-flowers.js`) the size of its
footprint, 1 m tall, **in the Building's own brown** (`#8d6e63`). For a fire pit that is a 2 × 2 m
brown box: a small shed. The 3D draws placeholders while the models load and keeps them if they
never do. **Needed from the owner**: the design file or a screenshot, and whether the bee hotel,
bee log or rain barrel in the same view looked right. Either way the placeholder should not look
like a building (a pale, low marker with the object's name), which F288 part 2 can take.
