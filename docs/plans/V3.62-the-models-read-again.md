# V3.62 · The plant models, read again against their photographs (F268)

**The owner:** "I'd like another ranking/rating of the 3d plant sprites against the photos like
previously only with the 'new' designs. This is to see which plants still need more work. Also
distinguishing between the different shrubs is a bit challenging so note ways to make them more
unique perhaps."

V3.48 read all 250 Edmonton natives against their iNaturalist photographs and scored them (mean
3.2, five of 250 at 7 or more). Since then the wildflowers (V3.48's second sitting), the shrubs
(V3.49), the trees and vines (V3.50) and the grasses, sedges and rushes (V3.51) were drawn again
from readings of those photographs, and V3.53 changed the light. V3.48's own plan says the scores
were never re-read after its second sitting: "a re-read is owed before any score is quoted". This
is that re-read. It is a review, not a fix: nothing in the viewer or the catalogue changes here.

## What was run

- The photographs fetched again from iNaturalist's open-data store (it answers from a session;
  the API does not, so the candidate list is still the owner's committed
  `data/fetched/photo_candidates.json`), six at a time.
- `model_references.py fetch`, `render`, `sheets`: every model drawn through the current viewer.
- Every sheet read and recorded with `model_review.py`, the same 0 to 10 question as V3.48:
  *would someone who knows this plant recognise it, and tell it from its neighbours?*

## Changed so the re-read is fair

- `model_review.py` keeps each plant's V3.48 reading under `earlier` when it is read again, and
  keeps its reference photographs when the new reading names none. `compare` prints this pass
  against the last.
- A sheet being read again no longer prints the old verdict in its corner (the reference
  photographs are still framed). Starting from a 3 makes a 3 easier to give.

## What still needs work, ranked

By plants reached, each a cause rather than a symptom, with what it looks like on the sheets:

1. **Leaf colour (88 plants).** One green per plant type for everything but the 47 graminoids
   (`scene_contract._FOLIAGE_BY_TYPE`; `leaf_color` was read only for the grasses in V3.51). Grey
   and silver plants (the sageworts, the pussytoes, silverweed, locoweed, arnica) are drawn
   green; blue-green ones (saskatoon, snowberry, dogbane, wheatgrass) a plain mid-green. Reading
   `leaf_color` off the photographs for the other 200 natives is a data pass on the V3.51 column,
   the cheapest change on this list for what it reaches.
2. **Daisies and small heads that do not read (49 flower_size, 14 flower_count).** Every aster
   (Lindley's, smooth, willow, many-flowered, marsh, western willow, greater northern, white
   prairie), the fleabanes, hawkweed, the sunflowers: their heads are drawn at life size on a
   0.5 to 1.2 m plant, seen mostly edge-on (`15-florets.js` tilts a `head` 0.18 to 0.36 rad from
   face-up), so a 2 cm daisy is a dot from 3 m. The woody blooms got a stated enlargement in
   V3.48; the herb heads did not. The asters are the single largest group still at 2 to 3.
3. **Dense flower architectures drawn as open strings (36 flower_arch).** Goldenrods (four at 3),
   milk-vetches, hedysarums, licorice, prairie clover, bergamot's pompoms, owl clover, giant
   hyssop: a spike, plume or thimble packed with florets is drawn as florets spaced along a
   line. V3.48's list had this fifth; it is still open.
4. **Mats drawn as a flat green twelve-sided plate (8 groundcovers, the bottom of the list).**
   Bog cranberry and creeping juniper (1), bearberry, twinflower, sweet-scented bedstraw,
   American brooklime (2), field chickweed (3). `03c-leafy.js` `_leafyUnderlay` puts a solid
   12-gon under a mat's sprigs, and from the side it is the whole picture. Creeping juniper is
   also drawn with broad blades, like a small agave.
5. **Vines that vanish into their host (5 vines, all 2 to 3).** Both peavines, wild vetch,
   blue clematis, twining honeysuckle: drawn on a host shrub, the pair reads as one dense shrub,
   and the vine's own flowers (purple vetch, orange honeysuckle) show as white specks. V3.50 gave
   read vines leafy shoots; on the sheets they cannot be told from the host's.
6. **Big compound or ternate leaves lying flat on the ground as one card (6 plants).** Wild
   sarsaparilla, the three meadow-rues, red baneberry, smooth sweet cicely: a 12 to 30 cm leaf is
   one flat cut-out on the ground or floating on a bare stalk, where the plant holds a lacy layer
   of leaflets up off the ground.
7. **Seed heads, the details (38).** The forms are right since V3.51; what is left is size
   (sedge spikes thin and smooth where they are fat and bristly), attitude (cotton-grass,
   Sprengel's sedge, woolly sedge should nod; they stand), and awns (needlegrasses, wild rye).
8. **Shrub fruit (24 shrubs).** See the shrubs below.
9. **Trees (10, none at 7).** Crowns too narrow and columnar on the birches, aspen and water
   birch; Jack Pine drawn with leaf cards (it reads as a poplar); tamarack as a dense spruce cone;
   bark grey where birch and aspen are chalk white; no catkins drawn on the April birches, aspen
   and alders despite `15c-woodybloom.js` (the April pictures show bare twigs).
10. **Water plants not yet given bodies (V3.48's open list, unchanged):** spiked water-milfoil,
    sago pondweed, bladderwort, buckbean, ivy-leaved duckweed, water parsnip, water arum;
    plus ostrich fern (2), which is still a bundle of stalks.
