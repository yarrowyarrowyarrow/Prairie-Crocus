# V3.55 · The year, and who it feeds (F270)

*The owner, 18 November 2026: "I'd like to review the Design tab and its subtabs. I want the
information to be useful without being superfluous or overwhelming. I'd like the order within the
subtabs to be such that the most useful info goes first. Imagine the typical user planning their
yard to grow some native plants and improve the ecosystem, what does this person actually need to
see and maybe more importantly what do they benefit from seeing. For example, the Food subtab, the
month by month is far and away the most useful information. Most people do not know or aim to
create a yard for a specific creature, perhaps there is a more informative, interesting,
captivating and simple way to weave in the data we have on animal species and their interactions
with the plants."*

## What the review found

Read off the real window on the worked example (V3.54's lot, 399 plants of 16 kinds), in the simple
and the advanced app (`scripts/surface_inventory.py --mode simple|advanced`).

- **The typical person never sees the month by month.** In the simple app Design is Overview and
  Planted. Food, Habitat, Over time, Water and the Report card are behind Advanced tools.
- **Food opens on a list of 1,603 animals**, set to whichever the design feeds most (the
  Brown-belted Bumble Bee), with tongue-fit chips, nesting and sources; the month by month is under
  the fold.
- **Two calendars.** Food's month by month and Over time's *This month* (a month picker, a headline,
  "go and check", a twelve-cell strip) answer the same question on two pages.
- **The Overview flags the owner's own strong example "Worth a look"** for "Few birds: only 1 kind
  eats its seeds or berries". The catalogue holds 55 fruit and 49 seed records in all, across 49
  birds; nearly every design is flagged. The flag measures the data, not the yard.
- **The best line in the tab is last on an advanced page**: "This design could raise a chickadee
  brood", under Habitat's checkboxes. It is also the true answer to "few birds": most birds raise
  their young on caterpillars.
- **Habitat** opens on the number and a fixed-width breakdown that prints database keys
  ("(bee_hotel, brush_pile)") and a broken sentence ("the other 1 are not known to support
  nothing").
- **The Report card** repeats the Overview (cost, signs of care) and Habitat (the score). Its one
  part found nowhere else is *Where to grow next*.
- **Water** asks five numbers before it answers.
- **Food says "in flower from April"** where the Overview says March (two plants flower in March):
  it printed the growing season's first month, not the design's.

Not this increment, queued as its own task: the first-year hours, 679–1,409 on the example, come
from a per-plant constant, so planting closer reads as more work.

## What the Design tab becomes

Simple app: **Overview · Through the year · Planted**. Advanced adds **Habitat · Over time · Water**.

### Through the year (was Food; `src/through_the_year.py` + `src/food_page.py`)

Top to bottom, the order the owner asked for:

1. **The year, month by month.** Twelve rows; each says in a line what that month holds ("6 in
   flower · 4 in fruit · 5 to harvest") and opens to: what is in flower, what is in fruit, what you
   can harvest (people kept apart and last among the foods, as before), **who is eating here**, and
   one thing to go and look at, with the month's jobs. The current month is open on arrival, and in
   a quiet month the first month ahead with something in flower or fruit too (*amended in the
   build*: in November the page opened on "November: resting", half the year's first impression).
   This replaces Over time's *This month* and its strip.
2. **Caterpillars to birds.** `chickadee_scenario` is the arithmetic, unchanged; the words are new
   and plain (no *keystone*, *larval host*), and the capacity is said as more or less than a brood,
   not as "106,500–284,000", which is a coarse proxy (150–400 caterpillars per host kind per plant)
   that the old line printed to the hundred.
3. **Animals that depend on a few plants**, and which of them the yard serves. From the documented
   `specificity = 'specialist'` records only (29 edges, ten animals: the Monarch on milkweed, two
   fritillaries on violets, the Snowberry Clearwing, the Yellow Warbler nesting in willows…), never
   inferred from an animal having few records in the catalogue, which is as often a gap in what is
   known (P9). Where the yard serves none, two that are recorded near the yard, with the plants
   that would.
4. **Look up any animal**: the old page's search, list and plan, unchanged, last.

**Who is eating here.** For each month, up to four animals, each a line naming the plant and what
it is eaten for that month ("Monarch: caterpillars on your Oval-leaf Milkweed"). Only animals with an
English name (WD-7), one species each (no *spp.*), food records only, active that month by the same
rule as the rest of the page (`what_it_feeds.months_for`), and, inside a region pack, **recorded near
the yard** (`fauna_fit` band *here*). Ranked: the pack's familiar animals first, one of each group
before a second of any, a caterpillar or a fruit or seed record before nectar, then most plants; an
animal shown last month waits behind one that was not, so the year is not the same four bees.

### The Overview (O7 amended)

Order: flowers through the season, food for wildlife, right plants, how full, from the street,
what it will take. The food item names up to three animals from *who is eating here* across the
year, keeps one count line ("recorded as food for N kinds of wildlife", one number, one source) and
the caterpillar line; it is *Worth a look* only when nothing is recorded eating the plants or the
caterpillar estimate is short of a brood, with a button to the plants that feed the most
caterpillars. The flowers and food items' links open *Through the year* without turning Advanced
tools on.

### Habitat (advanced)

Caterpillars to birds is on *Through the year* now; Habitat leads with the score and the lawn it
replaces, then **what would raise it** (the Report card's *Where to grow next*, moved), how sure we
are, the breakdown (database keys out, the broken sentence fixed), then the tools.

### Over time (advanced)

Year by year, then hours of work. *This month* went to *Through the year*.

### Water (advanced)

The answer first: what a dry week takes in the first summer and from year 3 (the weekly rule).
Then the numbers and the table. *Corrected in the build*: the review offered to say whether a
normal summer's rain covers the season. The page deliberately does not (R38, V3.20): a season can
total more rain than the plants use and still hold the two dry weeks that kill a new bed. The
answer is the weekly rule, which is what the page already concluded, now first.

## What the build found

- **"Go and look" was rewritten for the page.** The old *This month* line ("We predict Wild
  Strawberry and 4 more in bloom around now… notice what the model can't see") was the same for
  most months and spoke of "the model". Each month now names the plant that starts flowering or
  fruiting that month, else a plant to watch on a warm afternoon, else the first green, else the
  standing stems in snow (`through_the_year._look`).
- **Variety needs the whole year's memory.** With only "not shown last month", the same two
  animals alternated (Clouded Sulphur May, July, September). Ranking by times shown so far gives a
  different four most months on the example.
- **No bird is named month by month on the example.** Birds reach the list only through fruit and
  seed records (104 in the catalogue), and the example's fruit is not among them. The caterpillar
  story carries the birds, as the review argued.
- **A month is the plant's, not the animal's.** "Painted Lady: nectar from your Pussy Willow" in
  March is when the willow flowers; Painted Ladies reach Edmonton in May or June. The catalogue
  records a flight season for bees only. The note under the months says the month is when the plant
  offers the food. Reading the animals' own seasons is open.
- **Three Overview page tests had been red since V3.54**, all from the new example (it has no
  October gap, all four signs of care, and is three quarters covered, so those cards have no
  button to press). They now run each card's action as its button would.
- **The surface inventory draws a blank Overview here**, on V3.54 as well as on this branch, once
  `shapely` is installed; a script that fills the page and grabs it shows it whole. Not looked
  into further.
- `phenology_widget.py` went with *This month*; `phenology.py` stays (the API and this page).
- The Report card's blocks went with it: its cost by type, lawn tally and value-for-money lines.
  The Overview, Habitat and the PDF carry what of them a person reads.
