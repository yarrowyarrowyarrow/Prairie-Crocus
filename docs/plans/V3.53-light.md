# V3.53 · Light (3D-6, F265; takes F187)

*The owner, 17 November 2026: "go ahead with 3D-6, the light". The handover
([V3.43](V3.43-the-3d-preview-reviewed.md)): "Sky and ground light that follow the sun's height,
the ground bounce (F187), exposure that lifts at low sun and at night, the shadow map fitted to
the yard plus its casters' reach, soft contact shade under beds. Measured with V2.94's near-black
share over fixed views and hours in the yard probe; the owner approves from a before/after
sheet." That is decision D6, still open: **this look is the owner's to approve from the sheet
below**, and it is one chunk to take back out if not.*

## What the viewer did, read from the code

- **One hemisphere light with a fixed ground colour**, `0x5d6e51` at 0.9: 0.13 in luma, whatever
  the sun, the season or the ground. Anything turned from the sun (a wall, a trunk, a crown's
  underside, an animal's belly) is lit from below by that alone. F187 (V2.94) measured it at a
  seventh of what the viewer's own sun and sky put on its lawn.
- **A fixed exposure**, 1.15 by day and 1.0 at night. As the sun goes down the sun and sky
  weaken (by design, `updateAtmosphere`) and nothing compensates, so an August evening reads as
  dusk and the night as black.
- **The shadow map was a square round the origin** sized from `bounds`, the stage, which is
  floored at ±25 m: at least 75 m of map, 1,024 pixels across, for a 15 m lot, centred on the
  origin rather than on the yard.

## What was built (`html/scene3d/01e-light.js`)

One chunk, after `01d-season.js`, asked once per sun position (`relight`) and once per scene
(`fitShadowToYard`) by `permaSetScene` and `permaSetSun` through `placeShadow`
(`05-flowers.js`). Nothing runs per frame, and the shadow map's size in pixels is unchanged.

- **The ground sends back what falls on it.** The hemisphere's ground colour is the ground's
  reflectance times the light on level ground: the sun on the share of the ground it reaches
  (0.7, an estimate: things stand among things) plus the sky. Reflectance by what the season
  says the ground is doing (`seasonGround`): lawn 0.2, kept only faintly green; dormant grass
  0.22, straw; snow 0.75. So the up-light follows the sun's height and the season by itself,
  in the viewer's own light units: at the gate's noon 0.40 in luma (was 0.13), at a sun 8
  degrees up 0.14, over snow 0.71.
- **The sky's light warms with the horizon** as the sun goes down (up to 45% of the way to the
  horizon's colour).
- **The exposure follows the light on the ground** by its square root, from 1.15 at noon to at
  most 1.85, like a camera's automatic exposure that corrects only partly, on purpose: an
  evening stays darker and warmer than noon. At night 1.45, was 1.0.
- **The shadow map lies over what casts and receives in the yard**: the design's own extent
  (`scene.focus`) and every building and standing plant, their tops included, in the sun's own
  view, with 1.5 m of margin. A point at height and the spot its shadow lands on are one point
  in that view, so the casters' reach is in it. On the gate's yard 36 x 40 m at its sun and
  36 x 18 m at a low one, where the old square was at least 75 m. A scene with no focus (a
  walk, the sprite gallery), or one wider than 140 m, keeps the old square.

Every number above is an estimate nobody measured on a site, and the chunk says so, as V3.45's
fence heights do.

## Corrections made while building

- **The first reflectance was grass green** (0.16, 0.22, 0.10), and the first sheet put an olive
  cast on every wall turned from the sun: the house front at 6 and 7 pm read as painted. A lawn
  is blades, thatch and soil; the reflectance is now only faintly green (0.18, 0.21, 0.14).
- **The sun's target moved off the origin**, which `setSun` had reset on every call, and the
  up-light's first version read the sun's height from its position alone, which is only right
  with the target at the origin. Both read the direction from the target now.
- **The after sheet took 28 minutes, the before 5**, which looked like a per-frame cost. Timed
  pairwise on three pictures, the two viewers take the same time once warm (61 and 64 s); the
  first run of a pair pays for Chromium's start-up (166 and 440 s). Not the light.

## Left alone

- **Soft contact shade under beds.** Each plant already has its contact disc; a shade under a
  bed's edge would be geometry or a texture the ground does not have, and nothing measured asks
  for it. Left for 3D-7's beds.
- **The shadow map's resolution** (1,024) and its filter: a bigger map is a per-frame cost, and
  the handover says no per-frame cost is added blind before 3D-8 measures the laptop. The fit
  makes each pixel cover about a fifth of the ground it did on this lot.
- **The sky dome's colours** and **the fog**: they were already following the sun.
- **The conifers' dark recorded colours** (F187's note): the up-light lifts their undersides,
  their own colours are the models'.

## Not verified

- A graphics card. Software rendering changes light and edges more than anything else, and this
  increment is light: the owner's laptop is the real test, with the frame-rate readout
  (Ctrl+Shift+F) to confirm nothing got slower.
- Windows' QtWebEngine colour handling, which no session reaches.
