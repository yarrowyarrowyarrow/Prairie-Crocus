## What was measured first

The review lot (`scripts/render_yard.py`'s `review_lot`: 15.2 x 36.6 m, a scanned house, a
garage, two neighbours, two trees already there, planned front then back with the first-run
goals), as V3.51 plans it, under the preview's shade model:

| | By the fifth summer | By the tenth |
|---|---|---|
| Lost to shade | 14, all by the house | 39: 29 by the house, 10 by the plan's own tall wildflowers |
| Struggling (not trees) | 32 | 26 |

Every loss to the house is in the back bed north of it. Asked the shade model with only the
buildings and the trees already there casting, **35 of the plan's 365 plants stand in more shade
than their sun needs take**: Wild Strawberry 11, Purple-stemmed Tall Aster 10, Fireweed 6,
Prairie Goldenrod 4, Wild Vetch 2, Smooth Aster 2. Their shade is 0.78 to 1.0 (the model samples
nine sun moments, so shade comes in ninths); the most any of them takes is 0.7.

**Why the planner put them there.** It reads shade only through `_zone_context`, which needs an
elevation grid from the network and is all `None` without one (the offline user, this
container, and anyone whose fetch fails). Where the grid does come, its cells are 10 m, coarser
than the bed. The site's ranked list for these goals holds 22 plants that take full shade
(16 wildflowers, 2 groundcovers, 3 vines, 1 shrub), so there was always something to plant.

## What was decided

**Ask the preview's own model, not a second one.** The handover suggested a finer shade grid
from `shade.shade_grid_for_design(..., terrain=False)` feeding the cell scores. Two reasons
against: the grid is the 2D shade map's model, and V3.46's rule is that what shades a plant is
`SuccessionEngine`'s answer, never a re-derivation (the two differ in leaf weights and in how a
building is cast); and feeding the cell scores changes **every** plan, open yards included,
since a flat grid would replace the fallback positioner everywhere. The engine now takes
`fixed_only=True`, which leaves the placed plants out as casters, so its answer is the shade of
what does not grow.

**Trade the kind, keep the plan.** `src/plan_shade.py` runs last in the planner (after the
fill to cover, so the fill's plants are judged too). Each plant whose spot takes more fixed
shade, in its first summer, than its sun needs allow (`shade_ceiling`, the limit that kills it
in the preview) becomes a kind that takes that shade, **where it stands**: positions, drifts,
the plan's identity on each feature (`plan_run`, group, feature id) and its cover stay. The
choice:

- the same layer (`substitution.SUBSTITUTION_GROUPS`: a forb for a forb, a groundcover for a
  groundcover), of a similar height (a third of it to twice it, or half a metre taller), a
  taller one only when nothing of its size takes the shade;
- the plan's own kinds first, then the site's ranked list the plan was drawn from (the same
  goals, native here, pollinators);
- never a plant of standing water, never one the front yard's street band refuses, never one
  whose sun needs are not recorded (the engine is forgiving to those so as not to invent a
  death, which is no reason to plant one in shade, P9);
- **one kind per drift and shade tier**, so the shaded end of a drift reads as a drift of
  another plant, not a scatter. Part shade (up to 0.7) and deep shade are told apart, so part
  shade is not handed only deep-shade plants.

Where nothing takes the shade the plant is left out, and the plan says so (`shade_left`, a
*What I could not do* line). The review lot never needed it.

**What the person sees.** Each traded plant's page says why it is there ("Shaded by a building
for much of the day: a kind that takes shade", `why_here`). *About this plan* gains a line
(`plan_notes.DID`, a new table beside `COULD_NOT`): "Where a building shades the ground for much
of the day, it chose plants that take shade: Dwarf Raspberry, Lindley's Aster and Purple
Peavine." The advanced Design notes carry the English one.

**An open yard is untouched.** With no building and no tree already there, nothing runs, and a
test holds the plan to the one it was without the step. So the probe's 15 x 10 m open yard, and
every number it prints, did not move.

**Judged at the first summer.** A building's shade on a plant never falls as the plant grows,
unless the plant grows past the building, so the first summer is the most it stands in.

## Corrections made while building

- The handover set the gate as "under 10% shaded out by year 5". V3.46 had already brought that
  to 4% by fixing the model, so it was met before this increment started; the gate here is the
  one V3.46 proposed, none lost to a building's or an existing tree's shadow, made stricter:
  none in more of that shade than it takes, which also removes the struggling ones.
- The first build grouped a drift's misfits by the exact shade, which would have given one
  drift three replacements for 0.78, 0.89 and 1.0. Grouped by tier instead.

## What is left alone

- **Crowding by the plan's own plants.** The tenth summer's 10 losses are short plants beside the
  plan's Fireweed and Swamp Aster, and the three later ones the notes name as the house are the
  house and those plants together, neither enough alone (the house gives them 0.22 to 0.67
  against limits of 0.35 to 0.7). Tall wildflowers slowly crowding short ones is a thing that
  happens, and the preview says so; the planner's height gradient is the place to look if the
  owner wants it otherwise.
- **The cell scores and zones.** The scored placement still reads shade only from the elevation
  grid when one comes. The trade makes the plan right where it stands; moving the plan's
  layout toward the light is a larger change to every plan, not taken without the owner.
- **The shade map's sampling.** Nine sun moments a season (March, June and September at 9, 12
  and 15 h) is coarse, and is the preview's own; changing it changes the preview.
- The AI path (`generate_design`, the command line and MCP only) runs the same step, with the
  site-scoped catalogue as its list.
