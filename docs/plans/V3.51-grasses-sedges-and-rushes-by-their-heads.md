## What was read

The review's picked photographs, in strips
(`scripts/leaf_readings.py strips --group grasses`, a new group), for the 23
grasses, 15 sedges, 4 rushes and 5 grass-like water plants (cattail, great
bulrush, giant bur-reed, rat root, small bottle sedge): 47, all recorded with
`leaf_readings.py add`, `leaf_data_source = "photo"`.

**Nothing recorded said how a grass is held or what its head is**, so schema
v101 adds:

| column | what it says |
|---|---|
| `blade_width_mm` | a blade's width: 0.8 a Rocky Mountain fescue, 20 a cattail; none for a leafless rush |
| `blade_habit` | upright, arching, drooping, flat on the ground (white-grained mountain rice grass), or none (wire rush, great bulrush, three-square, the spikerushes) |
| `head_length_cm`, `head_width_cm` | the head's size: a panicle's length and spread, a sedge's cylinder, a rush's spray |
| `head_count` | heads a stem carries: a sedge's cylinders, a cotton-grass's tufts, a grama's combs |
| `head_color` | the head's colour in flower or fruit |
| `head_attitude` | erect, nodding, hanging (cyperus-like and Sprengel's sedges, cotton-grass) or from the stem's side below its tip (wire rush, the bulrushes, rat root) |
| `leaf_color` | the colour of a species' leaves as a photograph shows them in summer, for any plant (empty keeps the type's or genus's green) |

**`head_color` is not a flower colour.** Flower colours are the owner's
answers; a seed head's colour is a different trait, read into its own column,
and the website's colour facets, which read `flower_color`, are untouched.

## What was decided

**A read graminoid gets a body of its own, decided once in Python**
(`src/grass_habit.py`, a `drawn` block with body `tuft`), drawn by a new chunk
(`html/scene3d/03f-tufts.js`), in metres:

- **Blades** at their length and width, held as read, from one crown
  (`bunch`) or spread over the ground in shoots of four (`sod`). A blade
  narrower than 8 mm is drawn up to that width, at most four times its own
  (`TUFT_MIN_BLADE_M`): a fescue's 0.8 mm leaves are below a pixel at a
  yard's distance, and a tussock of them read as a few hairs.
- **A tall grass carries its leaves up the stem**: three on each flowering
  stem, and leafy shoots that do not flower (1.5 a flowering stem, to 55% of
  the height). Without them bluejoint and the manna grasses were a small
  basal tuft under a tall bare stand.
- **Flowering stems** as counted, as tall as the plant, the head inside the
  recorded height, a nodding stem arching over at its top.
- **Heads by form**, at the recorded size: spikelets in two rows for a spike;
  branches with spikelets at their ends for a panicle, hanging for a nodding
  one; a grama's combs held sideways; a needle grass's seeds with their long
  bent awns; a sedge's cylinders (with a slim male spike above when it has
  several), upright or hanging on threads; a rush's spray of small balls; a
  cattail's sausage and the thin spike above; a cotton-grass's white tufts; a
  bur-reed's spiky burs; rat root's finger jutting from the stalk.
- **A leafless rush or bulrush** is its stems, thicker for their height.
- **When the heads stand**, decided in Python from the bloom window: from
  flowering, as read for two months, then ripening toward straw, and held on
  the dry stems through the winter, except the cotton, the burs and the
  spadix (`SHED`), which do not stand the winter.
- Budget 4,000 triangles a plant at Balanced, 8,000 at Lifelike. A spray of a
  dozen flowers on each of twenty-five stems is drawn as fewer, larger balls
  where the allowance runs out.

**The pond defers** to this body for a read grass-like water plant, and
`buildPlants` names `tuft` in its routing (the V3.49 and V3.50 trap, held by
`test_grass_habit`). At Stylised a grass keeps the tuft its type always had.

**Leaf colour by species**, open since V3.48, now has a column, and the
scene's body colour reads it first. Only these 47 have one so far.

## Still left, and why

- **12 water plants**: arrowhead, water-plantain, buckbean, bladderwort,
  mare's-tail, floating marsh-marigold, ivy-leaved duckweed, sago pondweed,
  spiked water-milfoil, yellow pond-lily, water parsnip, and water arum. They
  keep the pond bodies V2.90 gave them (floating, submerged, broadleaf,
  whorled), which the review scored 3 to 7. What they need is different from
  a grass: real broad leaves on stalks, a pond-lily's globe, flowers standing
  clear of the water. That is the next sitting.
- **The four horsetails** (7 to 8 already) and **three specials**
  (brittle prickly-pear, creeping juniper, ostrich fern): each needs its own
  builder. The ostrich fern (2 of 10) and creeping juniper (2) are the worst
  left.
- **Leaf colour** for the 203 plants read before this increment.
- **Width**: as for the shrubs and trees, a plant is drawn as wide as the
  planner's spacing makes it, which is narrow for the sod-forming ones.

## Not verified

- **On a real GPU**: software rendering only.
- **In a yard of hundreds**: a grass is up to 8,000 triangles at Balanced
  where the baked tuft was a few hundred, instanced per species. A planting of
  60 grasses of six kinds is six meshes, but has not been measured on the
  owner's laptop (Ctrl+Shift+F shows frames a second).
- **My readings**: one reader, 250 px photographs. Blade widths in particular
  are an estimate against fingers and hands in the pictures.
